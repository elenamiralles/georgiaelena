About

I'm a mechanical engineer who likes the kind of work where technical rigor meets people, budgets, and deadlines.

I earned my B.S. in Mechanical Engineering from California Polytechnic State University, San Luis Obispo, where the Learn by Doing philosophy meant building and testing real systems from day one — everything from welding and GD&T to CAD and thermal systems design. That hands-on foundation is still how I approach problems.

After graduating I worked in environmental engineering at SCS Engineers, monitoring air quality and managing regulatory compliance data for state agency clients — a role that showed me how much technical decisions matter once they hit a real deadline and a real client. I later added a year of graduate coursework in Applied Mathematics at San Diego State University (PDEs, numerical methods, dynamical systems), which sharpened how I think about complex systems quantitatively — from soil heat transport to noisy, data-driven modeling.

I'm now focused on project engineering and environmental engineering/management roles, where I can bring together technical depth, organization, and the ability to work well with people to get something built, permitted, or delivered.

Off the clock: I paint, train for marathons, travel every chance I get, and pull espresso shots on weekends. More on that below.

Projects

Wind Turbine Rotor Design & Testing

Designed and fabricated a three-bladed wind turbine rotor for competition at the Cal Poly Wind Power Research Center. Applied blade element momentum theory to compute chord and twist distributions, then manufactured the blades from PVC using heat-forming techniques to approximate airfoil camber within tight material and budget constraints. The rotor successfully generated measurable power in field testing. The gap between predicted and measured performance prompted analysis of blade-to-blade consistency and the aerodynamic cost of fixed pitch.
Aerodynamics BEM Theory Fabrication Energy Systems Experimental Testing
View Project

Portable Ice Rink — Thermal Systems Design

Designed a portable refrigerated ice rink for Cal Poly Open House, modeling the full thermal system from heat load to hardware selection. Built a 2D finite difference conduction model in Excel to determine steady-state temperature distributions across ice, insulation, and concrete layers, and used Engineering Equation Solver to size PEX tube spacing, refrigerant temperatures, and flow rates for both an 8-hour and 24-hour freeze cycle. Selected pump and chiller hardware based on system curves and operating point analysis, and produced a full cost estimate for both designs.
Heat Transfer Finite Difference Methods Thermal Systems EES Systems Design Cost Estimating

Terrarium.jl Contribution — Soil Heat Diffusion Example

Contributed a validated 1D soil heat diffusion simulation to an open-source Julia package, verified against an analytical solution with sinusoidal surface forcing.
